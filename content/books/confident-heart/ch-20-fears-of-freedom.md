# Afterword — Fears of Freedom

## What this chapter explores

The resistance that meets anyone stepping outside societal norms around relationships. A lived encounter with collective defensiveness reveals the ego's architecture — [[shame-and-social-conditioning|individual fortress and shared cage]]. Guidance for navigating backlash closes the book.

## The message

Presenting [[networks-of-love|relationship networks]] as an alternative to monogamy met with fury and ridicule. The rawest response: it is just an excuse to sleep around. The vision centres on closeness. A stable network provides care and support, making you less dependent and safer overall.

These reactions come from inner fortresses. The ego defends the individual and the collective story — the cosy cage of societal norms. Its bars are "how things have always been" and its lock is the fear of the unknown. When someone escapes and suggests the door was open all along, severe cognitive dissonance follows. The insistence the cage is better is a desperate mantra justifying a repressed longing to fly. The anger stems from the terrifying realisation that imprisonment might be a choice.

You will meet these walls after your own inner work. Those in a fortress cannot be convinced by the beauty of open countryside, so do not waste energy arguing. Become a quiet invitation — embodied peace, joy and integrity awaken curiosity; your stability is the most compelling argument. Understand backlashes as positive signs: reaction intensity shows how much healing is possible. Find your flock — not the whole cage, but birds already listening for the song of the open sky.

The path of the confident heart is a pioneering one. It takes courage to face misunderstandings, gossip, and standing alone before finding your tribe. Even a cosy cage will always be a cage. Flying into the open sky embraces its freedom and uncertainties, where fearless peace, unconditional LOVE and openness live. Stay on track and keep flying.
