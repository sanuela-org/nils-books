# Ch 3 — Why Live Like a Potted Plant?

## What this chapter explores

On a park walk, Layla pitches the Network of LOVE as a garden rather than a potted plant. Maia pushes back with fears of chaos and loss. That evening, alone at her kitchen table, Maia writes out the vision in her own words. She is not yet convinced, but a root of curiosity has taken hold.

## In Essence

Under the park's soil a mycelial network connects every tree, bush and flower — sharing nutrients, sending warnings, the strong supporting the weak. That resilience is the model: when one partner needs time alone, others hold the space, and responsibility is shared rather than concentrated. No single partner can always meet every emotional and sensual need. Expecting one person to be best friend, cheerleader, therapist and lover at once is an impossible weight. Couples strain under it, then rumble — or fall asleep.

What the network is not: the dopamine chase, running through a garden picking every flower — destructive and lasting nothing. It is built slowly, on deep, stable connections and the care for them. Our fears come mostly from having no healthy models; anything else may feel unfamiliar and therefore unsafe. The biggest barrier is jealousy, rooted in old wounds and the fear of not being enough — explored further in *Love Beyond Jealousy*. Maia's body clenches at the thought of Mark holding another woman; it feels like a threat, the old programming.

She is not convinced — but for the first time she doesn't reject the idea. A tiny root of curiosity sits in fertile soil.
