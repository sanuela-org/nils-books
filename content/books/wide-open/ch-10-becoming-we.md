# Ch 10 — Becoming We

## What this chapter explores

Maia, Mark and Chloe share a heart-share and a hugging meditation. Jealousy and warmth coexist as two visitors in the same body. The chapter draws out non-attachment and compersion — skills for holding fear and joy together.

## The Perspective

Non-attachment means valuing each connection for what it uniquely offers, rather than measuring it against an idealised "should". Relationships evolve naturally; change is not failure. Releasing the urge to control outcomes and practising gratitude in the present builds resilience against anxious clinging. Non-attachment is not detachment: caring deeply without obsession or the demand that things last forever. Endings are held with grace, honouring what the connection was.

Compersion is not the absence of jealousy. It is the ability to embrace both fear and joy, ache and warmth. Jealousy is admitted without shame — suppressed, it only strengthens — and acknowledged as a signal, not a flaw. Practise it in low-stakes moments: a friend's delight, a stranger's laughter. That warmth in the chest is compersion, and it can be felt on purpose.

Ask what you are actually afraid of losing — almost always abandonment, inadequacy or scarcity. Ask whether that fear is rooted in reality or an old wound. Compersion is not numbness: active joy, not passive tolerance. Celebrate your partner's return as a choice, not an obligation: their happiness is evidence of their ability to LOVE, not a threat.

## Practice: Loving-Kindness for Compersion

The chapter closes with the practice: sit quietly, breathe through your heart centre, and inwardly recite kind wishes — first for your partner, then for yourself, and finally for their other connection.
