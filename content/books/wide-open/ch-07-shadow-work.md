# Ch 7 — Shadow Work

## What this chapter explores

A quiet evening unravels Mark's jealousy and envy when Maia takes a call from Dorian. What follows is his untangling of these feelings and an older wound. Maia responds with a generous offer that reframes the problem: not intimacy in the network, but a network still incomplete.

## Key Insight

Deep personal work is not optional for a sustainable, healthy [[networks-of-love|network]]. Discomfort is the price of growth — not a sign of failure, but evidence of real inner work. A complex Network of LOVE acts as a powerful mirror, reflecting unconscious patterns and unresolved wounds faster and more intensely than [[monogamy]]. When a strong reaction flares up, the intensity often marks an older, personal wound. Someone else's actions may stimulate a response, but the depth belongs to something older.

Jealousy, fear, envy and desire carry information. Meeting them with non-judgmental curiosity reveals unmet needs and boundaries rather than exposing shameful flaws. Core relational beliefs are triggered by network dynamics — beliefs like being unlovable, or fearing abandonment, or feeling that one's needs are a burden. Past trauma must be disentangled from the present so that current partners are met as they are, not as projections from old hurt.

Recurring patterns — people-pleasing, withdrawing, blaming — are automatic defences rooted in past experience. They can be recognised and broken. Self-validation is the deeper shift: reducing the dependency on external relationships to confirm self-worth, lovability, or stability. Committing to a lifelong practice of self-inquiry means each relational challenge becomes an opportunity for greater emotional freedom.
