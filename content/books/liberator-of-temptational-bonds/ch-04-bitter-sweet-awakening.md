# Ch 4 — Bitter-Sweet Awakening

## What this chapter explores

In early adulthood, Alistair's inherited relationship with wine and euphoric [[glossary#Euphoric Crystalglaze|crystalglaze]] surfaces as unease through mundane incidents, then as a seesaw between indulgence and growing concern. A family argument, dramatised in scene, detonates the emotions the substances were meant to numb.

## The Message

Alistair's private experiment: could he go five or seven days without wine, crystalglaze, sweet cornbread and noodles? Stress and romantic break-ups crumble that determination; each relapse ends in a promise the next stressful event proves wrong.

At learning circles, friends drink spring water with lemon instead of the long-banned alcohol, laughing from genuine joy. He laughs off questions, consuming wine and crystalglaze behind closed doors while spiritual teachings contradict the self-destructive philosophy he inherited.

A heated family argument bursts forth, as if years of suppressed emotions were catalysed by the very substances meant to numb them. Victor attacks Elara's parenting over crystalglaze, Elara accuses him of setting an equally poor example, and Alistair is silenced as too young to understand.

Like his parents, he has used substances to avoid hard truths; physical symptoms are hard to ignore: lingering tiredness, declining stamina, more illness. In the mirror he sees his parents' faces, a lineage etched in habits; he stands at a crossroads: let the substances dictate his life, or take the difficult steps to forge a new path.
