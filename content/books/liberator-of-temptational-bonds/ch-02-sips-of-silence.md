# Ch 2 — Sips of Silence

## What this chapter explores

Alistair's coming of age inside the Rosevale household, where his father's drinking and his mother's turn to [[glossary#Euphoric Crystalglaze|euphoric crystalglaze]]. Emotional avoidance, absorbed silently in domestic scenes, passes from one generation to the next.

## The Message

Emotional avoidance here is a family inheritance. Victor's eyes seemed glazed over with distance, and he waited for a change that never came; in the evenings he sat with newspaper and amber liquid, the first sip seeming to wash the stress lines from his face "as if the alcohol had the magical power to smooth out the wrinkles of his soul", before brooding energy silenced conversation.

While most families ventured into sunlight, Victor started his day with "the elixir of the fallen angels", coffee and whisky in a chipped mug, a comforting ritual that trapped him in repetitive emotional numbness. Elara cushioned the coldness from her partner with [[glossary#Euphoric Crystalglaze|euphoric crystalglaze]], a white, sweet, glittering substance, banned years ago, sold on black markets; its comfort an illusion. Neither parent spoke of their habits; Victor's toasts were a silent permission slip to indulge and avoid; conversation dwindled to ships passing in the night behind a wall of glass bottles. The spiral cost Victor friends and his job, while Elara seemed to be on a parallel path and their mutual denial left an emotional vacuum. The legacy passed to Alistair, who internalised the idea that alcohol could act as a balm for emotional discomfort and sneaked sips of Victor's wine and whisky, each a taste of the armour he thought he needed. Between child and adult he stood, heart yearning for more, hand still reaching for the bottle that promised a momentary escape.
