# Ch 3 — The Weight of False Comfort

## What this chapter explores

A family evening carries the weight of false comfort in miniature. Elara's lavish feast and her ornate box of [[glossary#Euphoric Crystalglaze|euphoric crystalglaze]] seemed to promise emotional fulfillment. What the scene delivers instead is distance, fatigue and the slow erosion of the bonds that should hold a family together. 
## The Message

For Elara, a full stomach felt like emotional fullness, every bite a layer of protective armour, and sprinkling euphoric crystalglaze over the candied fruit was her finishing fortification. Her true inner happiness and heartfelt laughter slipped away, leaving a hollow echo of the soul she used to be.

Victor's noncommittal nod and immediate reach for the wine carried him into a protective bubble, while young Alistair piled his plate despite a gnawing emptiness: what offered comfort can also imprison the soul.

Elara tried quitting her addiction many times: a few days of palpable discomfort and jittery emotions, then she returned back to her crystalline comfort. She urged Victor and Alistair to indulge too, as if she needed their participation to validate her own choices. 

Weight gain built a barrier between her and Victor, mirrors were avoided, and a practiced "Everything's great" covered the distance.

A fortress around her heart left her a distant observer of her life, feeling lonely even in a full room. Abstinence spiralled her mood into tantrums at Victor or Alistair, while forgetfulness, lethargy, persistent headaches and joint pain followed. Alistair felt relief and guilt as he came to understand his father's frustrations.

The comforts that once felt like LOVE now seemed like decorations on a collapsing structure, a substitution where cravings and self-love belonged. Years later, Alistair would realise where the foundations really began to crack.
