# Allowances and Boundaries

## Definition

Allowances and boundaries are the agreements partners make about what closeness, affection, time, and connection are welcome within a [[networks-of-love|relationship network]]. Unlike rules imposed on someone, boundaries arise from honest self-knowledge — everyone involved names their needs, comfort levels, and non-negotiables. Allowances name what is positively encouraged. Together they create safety. Because individuals and relationships evolve, these agreements are revisited regularly. 

## Allowances in a relationship network

The book [[soulfamilies|Soulfamilies]] describes it like this: when we no longer limit ourselves to being either 'single' or part of a traditional couple, new possibilities can unfold. Everyone is free to build their connections with care – setting personal allowances and clear boundaries based on their own and shared needs, always with the open knowing and acceptance of any closer partners involved. In this way, a network of LOVE begins to form naturally, with each soul connected to others through trust, presence, and shared experiences — the ground a [[soulfamily-community|soulfamily community]] grows from.

Respect for space belongs to the same understanding: knowing when someone needs quiet or alone time, without taking it personally. Soulfamilies names it as one of the values that often show up in strong, healthy groups. The rest of the common values, and the process groups use to shape agreements around them, are set out in [[soulfamily-compass|the soulfamily compass]].

## Positive agreements versus prohibitions

The most effective boundaries are framed as shared intentions, not lists of prohibitions. A prohibition says "Don't let another partner become too important." An agreement says "We will communicate proactively if a new relationship becomes serious, so we can discuss how our commitments, time, and emotional landscapes might adapt."

Translating restrictions into positive statements keeps the focus on what the relationship is building, not on what it fears. It also makes conversations about boundaries feel collaborative rather than defensive. The energy shifts from policing to co-creating.

- Proactively discuss and agree on freedoms, expressions of affection, and connections that are welcome, encouraged, and celebrated within each relationship.
- Identify personal comfort levels, needs, and non-negotiables regarding intimacy, time, and communication.
- Move beyond "don't" lists to reframe agreements as shared intentions and positive agreements.

## Setting boundaries together

Each partner's comfort zone is different. What feels safe to one may feel too open or too closed to another. The work is finding where two (or more) people's edges meet — and building the agreement there.

A common fear is that boundaries limit freedom. In reality, boundaries are what make freedom possible. Without them, anxiety fills the gap. With them, partners can relax into trust, knowing their needs are seen and respected.

Setting boundaries is not a solo exercise. Both partners sit down, share what they feel, and work out what would make a situation feel safe for each of them. The process itself builds trust — it proves that both people's feelings matter equally.

Before any new exploration, partners benefit from a deliberate conversation about boundaries — the [[planning-pause|planning pause]]. This is the moment to ask: What would make this okay for you? What do you need to feel safe? What are your limits?

This pause is about making sure both partner's feelings are considered before expectations form. Skipping it leads to resentment and misunderstanding. Honouring it demonstrates respect and builds the trust that makes exploration possible.

In one example, two partners facing a new exploration sat down and agreed on three clear boundaries: sensual and tender contact was welcome but nothing penetrative; the exploring partner would text when heading home; and no matter how late, they would do a [[reconnection-ritual|reconnection ritual]] afterward. These simple agreements gave both partners a sense of security. The boundaries themselves became a source of comfort.

## Documenting and communicating agreements

Agreements need to be stated explicitly. Unspoken assumptions create resentment. What one partner considers obvious may be invisible to the other. Agreements can be verbal or, for complex arrangements, written down. The point is clarity — both partners know exactly where they stand.

- Explicitly communicate your needs and limits using "I need," "I can," and "I cannot" as positive acts of clarity and self-care.
- Document agreements to prevent assumptions. Verbal works for simple things; writing suits more complex arrangements.
- Ensure both partners and everyone involved understands the agreement the same way before moving forward.

## Revisiting as relationships evolve

Agreements are not permanent contracts. Individuals grow. New connections form. Old comfort zones shift. What felt safe six months ago may need updating — because the relationship is alive.

Regular check-ins allow partners to revisit boundaries in a spirit of curiosity. The goal is to keep agreements comfortable, supportive, and aligned with where each partner actually is now. Changes should be treated as natural evolutions. Using the [[heart-centred-communication|heart-centred communication]] skills — honest, gentle expression from a centred place — ensures updates happen safely.

- Schedule regular check-ins to revisit allowances and boundaries as people and relationships evolve.
- Approach changes as natural evolutions rather than violations of trust.
- Use honest, loving communication to update agreements so everyone feels safe and respected.

## How this shows up

- A partner names that they need advance notice before new intimacy develops with a new connection.
- Two people agree to share significant experiences soon after they happen, while keeping everyday details private.
- A partner asks for a reconnection ritual after time apart, as a way to feel held.
- Boundaries around time, energy, and [[time-and-energy-management|scheduling]] prevent one person's exploration from unintentionally sidelining another's needs.
- Regular heart-share conversations surface small discomforts before they become large conflicts.
