# Clarifying Space Needs

## Definition

Before committing to a shared home, a [[soulfamily-community|soulfamily community]] clarifies what the space must support — function first, so the most beautiful place does not bring daily frustration. A space needs to feel right and carry warmth, harmony and possibilities. The group pauses and asks: Will this space support the life we want to live together? 

Shared living needs spaces that support connection and care, work and creativity, but also enough room for retreat, rest and privacy when needed. If both the right feeling and the practical use align, the group has found something rare and worth holding on to.

## What a shared space must support

The book [[soulfamilies|Soulfamilies]] describes the aspects to explore together like this:

- **What will we use this space for?** Start with function: What activities will take place in this space? You will need areas for living, sleeping, eating, working, gathering, creating, or simply resting. Naming these clearly helps the group see what kind of land, rooms and outdoor features you'll need, and which ones may not be necessary.
- **Must-have vs. nice-to-have features:** As a group, talk openly about what is truly essential and what would simply be nice to have. Some needs may be clear, like a shared kitchen or extra guest rooms. Others may be more flexible, such as a fireplace or a nice view. Creating this list together brings clarity and helps when [[shared-decision-making|making decisions]] later.
- **Individual vs. shared needs:** Every community has a mix of different personalities and rhythms. Some people will need more space for themselves. Others feel better in shared spaces. Talk about this early on: How can we satisfy both the "we" and the "I"?
- **Common space types:** In soulfamily spaces, certain rooms become central: a shared kitchen for meals and sharing, a living room for meeting, resting or cuddling, a corner for music or movement, and some quiet spaces to retreat to. Ask yourselves: Which spaces do we all need, and how often will they be used? These core spaces will shape how people connect and how they rest.
- **Private rooms vs. multipurpose spaces:** Many people want a private room, and there's value in that. But sometimes, space is limited. Multipurpose rooms can offer more flexibility: a room that serves as a yoga space in the morning could be a guest room in the evening. There could be a bed or two in the co-working space or in the creativity room. This only works if the group can agree on flexible times and is able to find compromises. The advantage is that you make better use of the same space and need less space overall.
- **Evaluating layout, room sizes, and flow of movement:** How people move around a house matters. Are essential rooms close together or spread apart? Can people pass without disturbing each other? Are rooms too small for group use or too large to feel cosy? A good layout makes daily life feel natural and reduces tension. Try to imagine a normal day in the house and walk through it step by step in your mind.
- **Planning for future growth:** Your group will change: others may join, children may arrive, or your shared activities may grow. Can this space evolve with you? Is there room to build something later – a yurt, a studio, a guest hut? Choosing a place with growth in mind gives you stability and space for the future.

## Shared sleeping as a space question

When a soulfamily first lives together under one roof, the living room can become the shared sleeping area at night. Each evening, a landscape of mattresses, blankets, pillows and duvets spreads out, creating a cosy space of comfort, safety and closeness. In ancient cultures and tribes, this shared rest was the natural way to sleep. It is only in our modern age of isolation that everyone seems to prefer being behind a closed door.

At first it all may feel strange and new, but soon rhythms and routines settle in. The quiet evenings before sleep can become a comforting ritual, sometimes with stories or music, sometimes just cuddling up together. To fully enjoy this shared nest of togetherness, everyone could put their phones away in the evening.

Over time, shared sleeping becomes sacred to the group: their collective sleeping space, a cocoon of shared trust and care. The book [[soulfamilies|Soulfamilies]] footnotes a blog post on shared sleeping: *Why We Think Sleeping in Isolated Rooms Is Normal* (sanuela.org/sharing-sleeping-place).
