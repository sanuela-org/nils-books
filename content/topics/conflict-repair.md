# Conflict Repair

## Definition

Conflict repair is the deliberate process of restoring trust and understanding after a disagreement. It treats conflict as a shared problem to solve, not a battle to win. The goal is to de-escalate tension, address the real harm, and rebuild safety so the relationship can continue to grow. Effective repair also accounts for the wider [[networks-of-love|relationship network]] — a rift between two people sends ripples to everyone connected to them, and the repair must consider those effects.

## De-escalation in the moment

Repair begins before the fight ends. When tension rises, both partners can pause rather than escalate. This means recognising when voices are rising, when defensive postures are forming, and choosing to step back rather than push forward.

The key tools are heart-centred breathing and [[heart-centred-communication|honest, loving communication]]. If either partner is triggered, the most effective move is to request a pause — not as withdrawal, but as a proactive tool for later clarity. A pause is not avoidance. It is a temporary retreat to regain composure so the conversation can continue productively. Being able to say "I need a moment" without shame is one of the strongest skills a partner can develop.

## Apologising for consequences, not just intentions

Many apologies miss their mark because they focus on defending intentions. Saying "I didn't mean to hurt you" can feel like minimisation when someone is in pain. Genuine repair addresses the actual impact — what happened and how it landed.

As described in the book [[wide-open|Wide Open]], an effective apology names the specific harm. It acknowledges that even well-meaning actions can cause real damage. This distinction matters because it shifts the conversation from who was right to what can be done to make things right. Forgiveness follows naturally when the one who was hurt feels truly seen and understood.

## Repair conversation vs. re-litigation

Rerunning the same arguments is a way of trying to determine who was wrong. A repair conversation moves toward healing. The difference is direction: re-litigation looks backwards, seeking vindication, while repair faces forward, seeking solutions and renewed trust.

To stay in repair mode, both partners can keep asking: "What do we need right now to feel safe again?" rather than "Let me explain why I was justified." The first question opens a door. The second one closes one.

## Timing and readiness

Repair works best when both partners are calm enough to listen. Attempting a repair conversation while still flooded with anger or hurt usually leads to more harm. This means acknowledging that the timing of a conversation matters as much as the content.

Small hurts that go unaddressed for days tend to harden into resentment. This is why catching concerns early, even through a short and informal daily [[heart-centred-communication#Practice: Daily heart-share|daily heart-share]], prevents many conflicts from reaching the repair stage at all. Regular, honest sharing keeps small issues visible before they become big ones.

## Ripple effects across the network

Conflict between two people does not remain confined to just those two people. Partners, metamours, close friends and others in the Network of LOVE can feel the strain; tension between one couple can affect the emotional climate of the whole group.

Repairing well means communicating with care about those ripple effects. It may involve acknowledging to others that something was difficult and is being worked through, rather than pretending everything is fine. Transparency about conflict without over-sharing helps the wider relationship network to maintain trust and stability.

Sometimes a balanced third perspective from within or outside the network can help mediate. The important distinction is healthy mediation versus triangulation, where a third party is pulled in to take sides or avoid direct communication. True mediation holds space for both voices without bias.
