# Shared Money and Resources

## Definition

In a [[soulfamily-community|soulfamily community]], shared money and resources are handled as a living agreement: something the group keeps talking and adjusting. Money can be one of the most sensitive issues in any kind of close relationship, especially when people start sharing daily life. It helps to talk about shared money and things early on, before tensions build up. The key is clarity, honesty, and space for different needs and situations.

## Small steps first

It often starts with simple things. Some soulfamilies choose to set up a shared monthly budget for meals, basic household items or shared activities, with everyone contributing what they can. Others keep track of shared costs using simple apps or a paper chart on the wall to stay transparent.

## Bigger questions over time

As the connection deepens, the questions get bigger. What if we rent a place together – who signs the contract? What if someone in the group already owns the home? How do we keep things fair? What if one member earns more – does that mean more say or responsibility?

These are always invitations for open and respectful talks. It often helps to write things down, even just informally, to keep mutual understanding alive.

## Keep talking

It helps to check in regularly: Is everyone still happy with the way we are sharing costs? Has anyone's financial situation changed? Do we need to adjust the way we do things?

Being open also means making space for different views on money. Some may want more structure, others more freedom. It's okay to disagree, as long as the conversation remains honest and kind.

## Practice: Money check-in

The book [[soulfamilies|Soulfamilies]] describes it like this: "Once a month, or when something bigger comes up, have a short money check-in: Everyone can share how they feel about the shared money situation. There is time to address small concerns before they turn into tensions and room to update the agreements if needed. It doesn't have to be heavy. A warm cup of tea, an open heart and the willingness to listen go a long way."

## Shared community income

Once the space, the practices, and the relationships within it are mature enough, the community can open its doors to others. One of the most natural ways for a community to sustain itself financially is by sharing what it has built.

Some guests may come for a weekend retreat – to experience [[inner-sanctuary#Practice: Heart-centred breathing|heart-centred breathing]], hugs or [[hugging-meditations|hugging meditations]], sharing circles, and the emotional warmth of conscious community life. Others may stay for a week or a month as co-living guests. They immerse themselves fully in daily life, contributing to shared tasks and exploring whether this way of living resonates with them. This is ideal for people who are considering starting their own soulfamily community one day.

Guests can contribute towards the community's shared costs: food, rent, energy and the work involved in hosting. In this way, a shared income arises quite naturally from the way the community already lives. The soulfamily shares a unique and deeply human way of life that can be passed on.
