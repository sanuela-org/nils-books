# Inner protector

## Definition

The inner protector is the mind's innate survival mechanism, often called the ego. It operates through prediction, avoidance, and overcoming of threat. Its mission is to guard a set of core fears: pain and loss, helplessness, abandonment, scarcity, failure, engulfment, being unlovable, shame, meaninglessness, inferiority, constraint, and fear of death. Its work was once life-saving and deserves gratitude, even while its strategies now cost us.

## The core fears and their strategies

The inner protector responds to each core fear with a distinct defensive strategy.

- Fear of not being enough → a hyper-competence persona driven by external achievement, status, and constant comparison.
- Fear of being unlovable → barriers against intimacy, pushing people away before they can reject you.
- Fear of being powerless → relentless attempts to control people, outcomes, and environment.
- Fear of being overwhelmed → numbing, intellectualising, retreating into the mind, and avoiding deep feelings.
- Fear of insignificance → a grandiose self-image, special and destined for greatness or uniquely victimised.

## The defence-mechanism self-assessment

The book offers this list as a self-check. Score each item from 1 ("I almost never do this") to 9 ("I do this constantly").

- Power games: the need to dominate, control, or submit to others in order to feel safer or more significant.
- Attachment: the deep, fear-driven clinging to specific people, possessions, ideas, or outcomes. This causes pain when things inevitably change and makes inner peace impossible until the attachment is released.
- Blame: the habit of accusing other people for causing one's own problems or pain in order to avoid taking personal responsibility.
- Lying: creating a false narrative or withholding important truths in order to maintain a sense of control over a situation, avoid shame, or protect one's self-image.
- Gossip: creating a false sense of connection and superiority by discussing the perceived flaws of absent people.
- Jealousy: the painful conviction that the happiness of somebody else diminishes one's own worth. (*)
- Shame: the painful fear that one is flawed, broken, or not good enough, leading to a desire to hide. (*2)
- Self-doubt: the inner fear that one's value or success is undeserved and that one is secretly a fraud. This creates a perpetual need to achieve and prove one's worth to feel safe.
- Defensiveness: the automatic reaction to protect one's self-image by rejecting criticism, no matter how valid it may be.
- The need to be right: prioritising victory in an argument over understanding and connection.
- Judgement and criticism: focusing on the flaws in others to avoid addressing one's own shortcomings and insecurities.
- Victimhood: adopting a passive and powerless identity, thereby avoiding the risk and responsibility of shaping one's own life.
- People-pleasing: sacrificing one's own values or needs in order to avoid conflict or gain external validation.
- Perfectionism: using rigid standards and relentless self-criticism as a shield against the fear of judgement and failure.
- Ignorance and denial: the avoidance or refusal to accept or see a perspective, fact, or feeling that is too painful, shameful, or threatening to one's beliefs or self-image.
- Manipulation: the indirect influencing or controlling of others to meet one's own needs, for example through guilt or deception.
- Comparison: measuring one's own worth against that of others ensures a constant state of either feeling inferior or feeling arrogant and superior.
- Greed/scarcity mindset: a fear-based belief that there is not enough for everyone.
- Grudges: holding on to past hurts to justify ongoing anger, refusal, or rejection.
- Impatience/irritability: an agitated resistance to the present moment and the flow of life arising from the ego's need for immediate gratification and control.

Add up all the numbers to find how active your inner protector is right now.

**Total of 70 or below:** your inner protector is a reliable and supportive advisor. The world often feels like a landscape of potential. You naturally feel a sense of internal spaciousness and curiosity.

**Total between 71 and 120:** your inner protector acts as a full-time manager. The world may often feel like a strategic game. You are in a state of heightened alertness, dedicating a large amount of your energy to scanning, planning, and maintaining a specific self-image.

**Total of 121 or above:** your inner protector stands guard constantly. The world feels like a territory that needs defending. You are in a constant state of high alert, directing your energy towards anticipated threats.

(*) More about jealousy in relationships: *Love Beyond Jealousy*.
(*2) This book explores some aspects of shame: *Luma's Garden. A Journey into Sacred Love, the Heart of Energy and Sensual Awakening*.

## From tyrant to advisor

The transformation goal is to shift the inner protector from a fearsome tyrant into a wise advisor. The fortress's identity stories and internal commandments are examined, not demolished. The inner protector is not destroyed but outgrown. The fortress stays in the landscape as shelter in a storm, not a permanent prison.

When the protector's voice as inner critic is met with gratitude rather than resistance, it turns back into strategic advice. The ego's identity stories are no longer believed as absolute truth but seen as patterns that once served a purpose. The destination is the unshakable self, where the heart leads and the mind serves it faithfully.
