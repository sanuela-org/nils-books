# Planning Pause

## Definition

The Planning Pause is a relationship practice: before committing to any plan that involves spending time with someone, pause and check in with your partner(s) first. This is not a request for permission. It is about transparency — ensuring no existing commitments or feelings are overlooked in the excitement of a new invitation.

## Origin

The Planning Pause grew out of a scheduling crash. One partner announced an evening plan without checking in. The other had a counter-proposal that went unspoken. Both assumed their time was flexible; neither asked. The result was not jealousy or betrayal — it was a failure to manage a finite resource: shared time. The conflict was painful precisely because it was preventable. A single check-in would have resolved it before resentment had a chance to take root.

## The Technique

- **The ten-second message.** Before finalising plans, send a quick message: "I'm thinking of meeting up with X on Y day. Would that work for you?" Ten seconds prevents scheduling surprises.
- **The 24-hour waiting period.** For significant firsts — visiting someone's home for the first time, staying over, or the first opportunity for sexual intimacy — allow a 24-hour gap between raising the idea and confirming the plan. This gives emotions a chance to settle and prevents decisions made in haste.
- **The shared glance.** When an invitation arises spontaneously in person, look at your partner before responding. A silent check-in that says: "We're a team, and your comfort matters to me."

## Why It Works

- It keeps the focus on transparency, not control. You are not asking "may I?" — you are offering "heads up, what do you think?"
- It surfaces unspoken expectations before they harden into resentment.
- It reinforces that everyone's time and feelings carry weight in the network.
- It is lightweight enough to use daily, and deliberate enough to protect significant moments.

## Common Misunderstandings

- The Planning Pause is not a veto system or a permission slip. It is an information exchange.
- It does not apply only to new connections. Existing plans and routines deserve the same courtesy.
- It is not about eliminating spontaneity. It is about letting your partner be part of the moment when it arises, even through a quick glance.
- Skipping it does not mean you do not care. It means the habit has not formed yet.

## Practices

- Build the ten-second message into your routine before every social commitment involving someone outside your primary relationship.
- Agree with your partner(s) on what counts as a "significant first" worthy of the 24-hour waiting period.
- When plans come up on the spot, make the shared glance automatic. It is a small gesture with outsized impact.
- Use the [[heart-centred-communication]] skills — especially 'I feel' statements — to voice discomfort when the Planning Pause is skipped, before it escalates.
