# Abundance and Scarcity in LOVE

## Definition

Scarcity thinking says LOVE is a limited resource: whatever one partner gives away is taken from you. Abundance thinking says the opposite — that the more LOVE flows, the more there is to give and receive. The frame you live inside determines whether a partner's other connections feel like a threat or like enrichment. Unlearning scarcity is one of the most consequential shifts that the Sanuela books address.

## Where scarcity thinking comes from

The book [[wide-open|Wide Open]] describes the scarcity mindset as a cultural construct. People believe that if a partner shares their attention, touch or tenderness with someone else, something is being taken away.

This belief fuels jealousy and the urge to possess. The scarcity frame makes any outside connection feel like a loss, even when nothing is actually missing. It shapes behaviour in quiet ways: monitoring a partner's time, feeling threatened by closeness with others, treating affection as a limited resource. People learn to shield LOVE rather than share it, and the relationship tightens around that fear.

## The economy of abundance

Why should we live like potted plants? A garden thrives through a [[networks-of-love|Network of LOVE]] where resources can flow naturally.

The difference is not volume but structure. LOVE Abundance is not about having more sex. It is about a wider web of mutual nourishment that makes each bond more resilient. When the network is healthy, each connection strengthens the others rather than competing with them.

When both partners express LOVE more openly with others by sharing physical comfort, cuddles or [[hugging-meditations|hugging meditations]], they return to each other fuller. A partner who comes home radiating warmth and wholeness carries abundance back into the bond. 

## How compersion proves abundance

[[non-attachment-and-compersion|Compersion]], the joy felt when a partner connects with others, isn't the absence of jealousy. It's the capacity to hold both fear and delight at the same time. When your partner's happiness feels like a gain for you rather than a loss, scarcity thinking dissolves through direct experience. You don't have to banish jealousy before you're allowed to feel joy for someone else. Both emotions can coexist.

## What scarcity thinking costs

- Jealousy intensifies and becomes controlling when fed by scarcity belief.
- Partners become unable to hold space for each other's growth.
- Touch and closeness become acts of possession rather than gifts of presence.
- The relationship becomes a closed system, making it rigid.
- Emotional burdens concentrate on two people instead of being shared across a network.

The scarcity mindset also shapes how people interpret a partner's independence. When spending time apart is seen as a sign of emotional distance and forming new friendships is perceived as competition, the relationship slowly contracts under its own weight.

Old wounds reinforce scarcity thinking. Understanding [[jealousy-as-a-team-process|jealousy as a signal]] rather than a verdict helps. So does distinguishing it from the [[shadow-work|deeper fears]] that drive it. Together these open the path toward abundance.
