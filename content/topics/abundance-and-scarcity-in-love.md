# Abundance and Scarcity in LOVE

## Definition

Scarcity thinking says LOVE is a limited resource: whatever one partner gives away is taken from you. Abundance thinking says the opposite — that the more LOVE flows outward, the more there is to give and receive. This is not a small difference. The frame you live inside determines whether a partner's other connections feel like a threat or like enrichment. Unlearning scarcity is one of the most consequential shifts the book addresses.

## Where scarcity thinking comes from

The scarcity frame is cultural programming, not a fact about LOVE. People absorb the idea that if a partner shares attention, touch or tenderness with someone else, something is being subtracted.

This belief fuels jealousy and the urge to possess. The scarcity frame makes any outside connection feel like a loss, even when nothing is actually being taken. It shapes behaviour in quiet ways: monitoring a partner's time, feeling threatened by closeness with others, treating affection as a zero-sum currency. People learn to guard LOVE rather than share it, and the relationship contracts around that fear.

The book [[books/wide-open/wide-open|*Wide Open*]] describes it like this: we have been taught that LOVE given to one lover takes something away from another. This is something we have been taught, not something we have proven.

## The economy of abundance

A potted plant depends entirely on a single caregiver. A garden thrives through a [[networks-of-love|Network of LOVE]] where resources flow between many living things.

The difference is not volume but structure. Abundance is not about having more partners or more sex. It is about a wider web of mutual nourishment that makes each bond more resilient. When the network is healthy, each connection strengthens the others rather than competing with them.

When both partners give LOVE outward, they return to each other fuller rather than depleted. The wider flow does not drain the relationship — it feeds it. A partner who comes home radiating warmth and wholeness carries abundance back into the bond. Giving LOVE away does not diminish it; it creates more.

## How compersion proves abundance

Compersion — the felt joy in a partner's other connections — is the experiential proof that LOVE multiplies rather than divides. It requires practice: noticing authentic happiness in low-stakes moments, then learning to hold jealousy and warmth at the same time.

[[non-attachment-and-compersion|Compersion]] is not the absence of jealousy. It is the ability to embrace both fear and joy simultaneously. When a partner's joy becomes your own expansion instead of your own loss, scarcity thinking dissolves through direct experience. You do not need to overcome jealousy before being allowed to feel joy for someone else. Both feelings can coexist, and that coexistence is itself proof that LOVE is not running out.

## What scarcity thinking costs

- Jealousy intensifies and becomes controlling when fed by scarcity belief
- Partners become unable to hold space for each other's growth
- Touch and closeness become acts of possession rather than gifts of presence
- The relationship becomes a closed system — brittle and unsustainable
- Emotional burdens concentrate on two people instead of being shared across a network

The scarcity frame also shapes how people interpret a partner's independence. Time apart becomes evidence of emotional distance. New friendships feel like competition. The relationship slowly contracts under the weight of a story that was never true.

Old wounds reinforce scarcity thinking. Understanding [[jealousy-as-a-team-process|jealousy as a signal]] rather than a verdict helps. So does distinguishing it from the deeper [[shadow-work|fears]] that drive it. Together these open the path toward abundance.
