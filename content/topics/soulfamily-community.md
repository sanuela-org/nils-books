# Soulfamily Community

## Definition

A soulfamily community is a chosen family: a small circle of trusted souls who share daily life, care and growth. It grows outward like [[networks-of-love|the mycelial network]] that feeds a forest, rather than inward like the closed couple box. The longing it answers is for belonging itself: people who trust one another, are there for one another, and remain closely connected, no matter what life brings. Children are cared for collectively by several adults; what that offers parents, members and children alike is set out in [[raising-children-together|raising children together]].

## The visioning questions

Before you begin, take time to be alone, feel what truly matters to you, and remember what kind of life you long for and what kind of LOVE you are ready to receive and give.

Below is a list of guiding questions. There are no right or wrong answers, only honest ones. The aim is to listen to your heart and imagine what kind of future feels most right for you.

Take your time, let the questions sink in and stir your feelings. You may wish to come back to some of them later. Your vision will evolve as you do. Write down your thoughts, or draw a mind map if that helps.

- What kind of home do I feel good in – and who do I want to share it with?
- How much solitude and how much closeness feels right for me?
- What does an ideal daily rhythm look like?
- What values are most important to me when living with others?
- What is my LOVE language? How do I like to be loved and to feel cared for?
- How do I want to receive and give support when things get challenging?
- What kind of sensual connection and intimacy do I long for?
- What are my emotional needs and how can I express them clearly?
- What old fears or patterns might block me from being fully honest or open?
- Where in my life have I been holding back from deeper connection, and why?
- What would it feel like to raise children together in a small circle rather than as a couple?
- Am I ready to be seen, fully, in a small, close soulfamily?
- What kind of agreements, allowances and boundaries would help me feel both safe and free?
- What would it feel like to truly belong – and to offer that belonging to others?

Questions about agreements and boundaries point toward the [[soulfamily-compass|soulfamily compass]] a community builds together, and the decisions that flow from it toward [[shared-decision-making|shared decision making]].

## A home with open doors

A soulfamily community does not require everyone to stay forever. While some people may form the stable, long-term core of the community, a wider circle of close 'soulfriends' may come, staying for a few months before travelling or living elsewhere for a while and then returning.

This is the natural rhythm of modern life, particularly for young people who are still exploring where and how they want to live. What matters is the quality of the bonds – the trust, care, and shared understanding that make someone feel like part of the family, whether they are present or far away.

In this way, the core community can consist of between three and six people at any given time. There could also be additional space to host volunteers, short-term retreat guests, or people who wish to experience conscious community living for a week or a month. These guests would contribute to a shared community income and bring fresh energies. For how money and resources can be handled in practice, see [[shared-money-and-resources|shared money and resources]].

When the search for a shared home begins, the practical questions take over: what a space must support is worked through in [[clarifying-space-needs|clarifying space needs]].

## Practice: First steps

The book [[soulfamilies|Soulfamilies]] describes it like this: it doesn't have to start with the perfect house in the countryside or a fully formed plan. It can grow with a feeling, a conversation, or even just one message. A soulfamily doesn't form all at once. It's woven step by step, moment by moment, through care, trust, and shared direction. Here are some simple ways to begin:

1. **Reach Out:** Think of one or two people in your life who might be open to exploring deeper connection, shared values, or living more closely. Start a small group chat or invite them for tea. Share the *Soulfamilies* book, or simply ask: *Would you be open to dreaming together about a new way of living and supporting each other?*
2. **Plan a Weekend Together:** Choose a time to gather, even just for two nights. Share meals, talk about dreams and needs, reflect on how it feels to live close for a few days. You might sleep in the same room, do a creative project or have sharing circles.
3. **Write Down Your Own Values:** Set aside a quiet hour to write down what is really important to you in relationships and shared living. What kind of emotional space do you long for? What makes you feel supported, safe, free and inspired? Then, invite others to do the same and share what you wrote.
4. **Make a List Together:** Create a shared document with important practical and emotional topics: food, money, space, closeness, conflict, parenting, guests, ... It doesn't have to be perfect, it's just the beginning of a shared direction.
5. **Be Honest About the Edges:** Notice where your fears, hesitations or resistance show up. Gently bring them into the conversation. Starting something new means being willing to face what's true for you and move forward.

Above all, don't wait for perfection. No place, no plan, no moment will ever feel fully complete. Start with what is there – the people, the feelings, the longings. Soulfamilies grow through trust, not through certainty. And trust begins with the first step.
