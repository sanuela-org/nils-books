# Soulfamily Compass

## Definition

A [[soulfamily-community|soulfamily community]] is a small group of souls that chooses to share decisions, daily rhythms, emotional spaces and LOVE. Its agreements are a living compass that holds the group's shared sense of what matters most, growing and adapting as the relationships deepen and new experiences are made.

The soulfamily compass is about co-creating a shared ground where everyone feels seen and supported. Making the agreements visible replaces silent assumptions. Emotional agreements — speaking from your own experience, [[growing-with-triggers|owning your triggers]] and [[emotional-self-regulation|reactions]], checking in before offering feedback — guide daily speech.

## Common values

The book [[soulfamilies|Soulfamilies]] describes it like this:

Each soulfamily is different, but here are some values that often show up in strong, healthy groups:

- **Transparency.** Open, loving communication, being honest about feelings and needs, even when it's uncomfortable.
- **Mutual Care.** Not keeping count, but knowing that the soulfamily looks out for one another.
- **Support in LOVE.** Caring for each other's connections, not trying to control or wanting to possess.
- **Emotional Responsibility.** Being aware of our own triggers, and not placing blame on others.
- **Freedom.** Space for each one to choose, grow and follow their own inner guidance.
- **Respect for Space.** Knowing when someone needs quiet or alone time, without taking it personally.
- **Commitment to Growth.** A willingness to heal, reflect, and keep learning together.
- **Shared Intentions.** Clarity about why we choose to live together and what we want to create.
- **Celebrating Differences.** Seeing each soul as unique and knowing this diversity makes us stronger.
- **Staying With the Process.** Not running away when things get difficult, but slowing down and listening more deeply.

## The compass process

The best way to shape agreements is to let every voice be heard with care and presence. At [[sanuela-retreats|Sanuela Retreats]], you can be personally guided through this work. Here is a process that many groups find helpful:

1. **Start Alone.** Take time on your own to reflect: What do I need to feel safe? What do I long for when I share my life and my LOVE? What feels non-negotiable to me?
2. **Small Circles.** Meet in pairs or trios to listen and share. Sometimes we can hear ourselves more clearly when someone else is listening.
3. **Come Together.** Share your lists, wishes and needs with the full group. Notice where things match and where they don't. Both are important.
4. **Make a Mindmap.** Rather than a fixed list, create a visual compass – a mindmap with values in the centre, and expressions, notes and examples around them. Hang it on the wall when you come together for a shared weekend or trial time. Update it as needed. Let it live and breathe with you.

## Making agreements visible

When an unspoken agreement is exposed, the group starts making a list together — not just about the situation that exposed it. The list gathers all the things that might need shared agreements: finances, guests, cleaning rhythms, space for solitude, shared meals, sensual boundaries, emotional support, and more. It becomes a long list of topics. On a large sheet of paper, the topics can be grouped into broader categories. For each area, the group can agree on which [[shared-decision-making|decision-making model]] feels best.

The lists could hang on the kitchen wall as a living reference point. Sticky notes are added, things are moved around, and the wording changes as the group discovers more layers. It is all about making things visible — no more relying on silent assumptions.

A regular check-in rhythm grounds these agreements. The group may agree to meet in a sharing circle at least every other day, even when there is no particular reason to do so. The point is to stay connected, to share and to listen, even if only for ten minutes. Each meeting makes space for what might otherwise remain unsaid.

Some meetings are quiet, even wordless, just holding hands and feeling the presence of the soulfamily. Other times, someone cries or asks for support for an emotional process they are experiencing. The steadiness of these gatherings build trust: living together means being willing to talk about conflicts before they create barriers between the members.

## Emotional agreements

The book [[soulfamilies|Soulfamilies]] describes an established community two hours away that has lived together for over a decade. One of its members described the value of emotional agreements — shared understandings that serve as the community's rules of daily speech:

- We speak from our own experience.
- We take responsibility for our triggers and our reactions.
- We agree to check in before offering feedback.

Taking responsibility for your triggers and reactions is the daily work of [[emotional-self-regulation|emotional self-regulation]].
