# Networks of LOVE

## Definition

A Network of LOVE is a stable, honest web of relationships that shares the emotional load a couple would otherwise carry alone.

Its image is a potted plant set against a garden. The potted plant is completely dependent: if the caregiver is too busy or too tired, it suffers, isolated with little other support. A garden is joined underground by a mycelial network connecting every tree, bush and flower — sharing nutrients, sending warnings, letting the strong support the weak. That resilience is the model: when one partner needs time alone, others hold the space, and shared responsibility takes the pressure off.

> "Why live like a potted plant when we could also live in a rich garden?"

Expecting one person to be best friend, cheerleader, therapist and lover at once is an impossible weight; under it couples strain, then things rumble — or fall asleep. Wanting more closeness than one partnership can offer does not mean something is missing from the main relationship. LOVE is abundant, not a limited resource: that LOVE given to one lover takes something away from another is something we have been taught, not something we have proven. Touch is not a claim of ownership — closeness can share comfort, LOVE and healing without possessing anyone. The hugging meditations work this way: touch as a gift of deeply loving presence rather than possession, its wholeness and warmth carried home into another bond.

What it rejects is the dopamine chase: running through a garden picking every flower. Growth, not appetite:

> "It's not about having more sex. It's about experiencing more growth!"

Slow, honest, grounded growth is what makes a network more enriching and stabilising than monogamy. Jealousy is the biggest barrier — rooted in old wounds, in the fear of loss or the feeling of not being enough, fear rather than actual loss. Understanding it comes before going further, and *Love Beyond Jealousy* takes it up.

## Common misunderstandings

- **"It's a pitch for more sex."** It is about growth, not appetite; the dopamine chase through a garden picking every flower is exactly what it rejects.
- **"Touch implies a claim to ownership."** The idea is deeply ingrained in cultural stories, and it is exactly what the model unwinds: closeness can share comfort and healing without possessing anyone.
- **"Jealousy proves the model wrong."** Jealousy is the barrier to understand first, not a verdict on the model. It is rooted in old wounds, in the fear of loss or the feeling of not being enough, and it grows mostly out of having no healthy examples — knowing only monogamy or chaotic dating, anything else may feel unfamiliar and therefore unsafe at first.

## How this shows up

- Hugging meditations, where touch shares comfort without possessing anyone.
- Holding the space when a partner needs time alone, so care is carried by more than two people.
- Carrying warmth home from one connection into another.
- Wanting more closeness without reading it as something missing from the main relationship.
