# Safety and Consent

## Definition

Safety and consent are the ground every other skill stands on. Consent is not a one-time permission given before an encounter. It is continuous, revocable, and present at every stage of contact — from the first touch to the final farewell. A safe word or stop signal lets anyone pause or end an experience at any moment without guilt or explanation. Clear rules make openness possible rather than reckless. The book [[books/wide-open/wide-open|*Wide Open*]] describes it like this: trust, consent and fairness stabilise the whole circle.

## Consent as Continuous and Revocable

Consent given at the start does not lock anyone in. Each moment of closeness carries its own yes or no. Partners check in — sometimes with words, sometimes with a shift in breathing or posture — and the response is always honoured immediately. A no spoken today does not cancel the yes of yesterday, and a yes today does not bind tomorrow. This fluidity is what keeps contact alive rather than contractual.

- Consent is renewed at every stage of contact, not assumed from a prior agreement.
- Anyone can pause or stop at any time with a safe word, signal, or simple "no."
- A refusal carries no guilt, no obligation to explain, and no consequence for the relationship.
- Partners read body language as carefully as they listen to spoken words.

## Safe Words and Stop Signals

A safe word is a pre-agreed word or phrase that ends contact instantly. Some circles use a traffic-light system: green for comfort, yellow for caution or slowing down, red for full stop. Others choose a word that would never arise naturally in the moment. The mechanism matters less than the commitment behind it — when a safe word is spoken, everything stops, no questions asked, no feelings hurt.

- Choose a safe word before any intimate or vulnerable practice begins.
- The traffic-light system (green / yellow / red) offers graduated options.
- A stop signal can also be non-verbal: a tap, a squeeze, a shift away.
- Honouring a stop signal without hesitation is the deepest act of care.

## Safety as the Foundation

Opening wide does not mean giving up safety. Safety is built differently in a [[networks-of-love|network of LOVE]] than in a closed couple — it is woven through clear [[allowances-and-boundaries|agreements]], regular [[planning-pause|check-ins]], and the willingness to sit with discomfort rather than act on impulse. When partners feel safe, touch and closeness flourish naturally. Without safety, even the gentlest contact triggers guardedness and resentment.

- Safety enables the vulnerability that makes real connection possible.
- Clear agreements about what is welcome and what is not remove anxiety.
- A [[planning-pause|transparent check-in]] before any significant first ensures everyone's feelings are considered.
- Revisiting agreements as relationships evolve keeps safety current, not frozen.

## Trust, Consent, and Fairness Stabilise the Circle

When partners offer a steady presence, honest communication, individual allowances, and a shared commitment, they create a calm and resilient field. Touch, closeness, and even arousal can flourish without fear because trust, consent and fairness stabilise the whole circle. This is not a rule to memorise but a living practice — each act of honesty and each honoured boundary adds another thread to the web of safety.

## How This Shows Up

- A partner pauses mid-embrace to ask, "Is this still okay?" and the question feels natural.
- Someone uses a safe word and the other stops instantly, with warmth and no resentment.
- Before a new level of closeness, partners sit down and name what would make it feel safe for everyone.
- A felt sense of unease is treated as information, not as a problem to push through.
- After time apart, partners reconnect through a ritual that restores the sense of being held.
