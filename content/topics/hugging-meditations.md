# Hugging Meditations

## Definition

A hugging meditation is a sustained, fully clothed embrace between two or more people, practised face to face with eyes closed and breathing through the heart centre. The practice is not sexual and not casual. It is a deliberate act of energetic healing — a way of opening the chest, releasing stored tension, and generating a radiant inner glow that participants carry with them long after the embrace ends. Touch is offered as a gift, not claimed as possession. The practice requires clear rules, a safe word, and full consent.

## The Practice

Sit facing your partner. Close your eyes. Begin by holding hands — this is enough to start feeling the energy between you. A gentle current flows from palms and presence, travelling through the body like soft humming warmth.

When both feel ready, move into a closer embrace. One option is sitting in yab yum: thighs wrapped around the partner's hips, chests touching, arms around each other, heads resting together. Find the position that feels natural and safe.

Breathe naturally, but direct the breath through the centre of your chest. Imagine breathing pure LOVE in and out. Nothing else matters right now. Just feel.

The initial awkwardness dissolves. What remains is deep, beautiful presence. A wide-open feeling spreads from the chest — not excitement or romantic attraction, but a vibrant, loving peace. Like dancing liquid light, it moves through every cell, releasing tension you did not know you were carrying.

Time softens and stretches. Five minutes or twenty — it does not matter. When the practice feels complete, separate slowly. There is no need for words. The shared presence speaks for itself.

## Safety and Structure

Every hugging meditation rests on clear agreements:

- Eyes stay closed. This keeps the experience inward rather than performative.
- Bodies remain fully clothed. Clothing is removed only if both partners explicitly choose to, and only within a pre-agreed context.
- A safe word exists. Either person can end the embrace at any time, for any reason, without explanation.
- There is no agenda. Neither partner expects something from the other — no conversation, no reciprocity, no romantic escalation.
- The practice is consensual at every stage. One person guides the other closer only after feeling genuine openness and willingness.

## The Glow

The body responds to hugging meditations with a specific sensation: a warm, golden light radiating from the chest. This is not metaphor. Participants describe it as real, physical, and deeply nourishing — like a spa for the soul.

The glow stays with you after the practice ends. It follows you home. It changes the way you see your surroundings and the people in them. Carrying this warmth into another bond — a primary partnership, a friendship, even a moment of ordinary connection — is part of how the practice heals.

Touch withheld by the conditioned social system is one of the deepest deprivations people experience. A hugging meditation restores what culture has taken: the simple, sacred act of holding another person without needing to own them.

## Carrying It Home

The practice does not stay in the circle. Its purpose is to nourish every bond you have. When you return to a partner after a hugging meditation, you bring a fuller heart and a calmer presence. Sharing the experience openly — describing the glow, the safety, the feeling of being seen — builds trust rather than breaking it.

Holding hands in everyday life becomes a mini-hug once you have felt the full practice. What was once a symbol of mutual ownership transforms into a small act of energetic connection.

## Reconnection After the Practice

After time apart — especially if one partner has been with someone else — a reconnection ritual bridges the gap. The partner who stayed home may have been alone with worry. The returning partner still carries the energies of their time with another person.

Welcoming practices include sitting face to face and breathing together while holding hands, asking "How do you really feel?", or embracing for ten minutes in yab yum. The specifics matter less than the commitment: don't skip it. The reconnection ritual is the glue that turns absence and longing into deep appreciation.
