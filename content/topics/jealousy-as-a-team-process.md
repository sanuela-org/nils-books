# Jealousy as a Team Process

## Definition

Jealousy is not a personal failing or a sign that LOVE is being tested. It is a fear alarm — a signal that an old wound, an insecurity, or an unmet need has been activated. In a [[networks-of-love|Network of LOVE]], jealousy is the shared responsibility of everyone involved. All affected people acknowledge the feeling, sit with it together, and work collaboratively to find safety and restore stability. The team works with fears, insecurities and pain as one unit, rather than leaving anyone alone with the storm. This is the model's most challenging skill and the one that takes the most practice.

## Why jealousy hits networks harder

A relationship network acts as a powerful mirror. It reflects unconscious patterns, insecurities and unresolved wounds faster and more intensely than a closed couple relationship ever could. Every new connection, every moment of joy shared with someone else, can trigger old fears — the fear of being replaced, of not being special, of being used for stability while the real excitement happens elsewhere.

- The traditional model hides jealousy behind prohibitions and possessiveness. The network model asks everyone to face it openly.
- When someone's joy triggers another's fear, the feeling can spiral quickly if it is left unspoken.
- The fear is almost always about loss — loss of a special role, of attention, of safety — not about the actual event.
- Without healthy models of jealousy being handled well, people default to the only scripts they know: control or withdrawal.

## What jealousy is rooted in

- **Old wounds.** Jealousy is rarely about the present moment alone. It is fear wearing a mask, and underneath that mask is almost always a fear of abandonment, inadequacy or scarcity. Past betrayals, childhood attachment wounds, and experiences of being used or left behind colour how present events are felt.
- **Fear of loss.** The core thought is: *I'm losing her. I'm losing him. I'm losing us.* But the feeling often activates before any loss has actually occurred — it is a projection of dread, not a response to reality.
- **Fear of not being enough.** A partner's joy with someone else can trigger the belief: *I am the safe, predictable one. The real, exciting connection is happening somewhere else. My value lies in my stability, and my punishment for being stable is being boring.*
- **Envy tangled with jealousy.** Jealousy says, *I'm losing something I have.* Envy asks, *Why does she get to have that and I don't?* The two feelings often twist together into a single knot. Untangling them is part of the work.
- **No healthy models.** People have only ever known [[monogamy]] or chaotic dating. Anything else feels unfamiliar and therefore unsafe. The absence of examples makes jealousy feel like proof that the whole model is dangerous.

## Working jealousy as a team

The team-process approach transforms jealousy from a shameful failing into a shared puzzle to solve. No one is left alone with the monster in their stomach. The book [[books/wide-open/wide-open|*Wide Open*]] describes it like this:

- **Acknowledge the signal.** When jealousy arises, say it out loud. Suppressing it only strengthens it. "I feel jealous right now. That's perfectly normal." The goal is not to eliminate the feeling but to stop it from running the show.
- **Sit with it together.** Everyone affected breathes through the heart centre and listens. The one feeling jealousy is not being accused. The one triggering it is not being blamed. The team is working to understand the root cause together.
- **Identify the real fear.** Ask: *What am I afraid of losing?* Once named — abandonment, replacement, invisibility — separate the fear from the present reality. Often the fear is rooted in an old wound, not in what is actually happening.
- **Let jealousy and joy coexist.** Jealousy and compersion can happen at the same time. Both are real. Both are valid. The one feeling jealousy does not need to overcome it before being allowed to feel joy. Naming both — *I feel jealous, and I'm also truly happy for you* — is deeply liberating.
- **Restore trust through action.** After the feeling has been heard and validated, the team returns to its shared agreements: check-ins, transparency, the [[allowances-and-boundaries|allowances and boundaries]] that protect everyone's safety. Trust is rebuilt not by eliminating jealousy but by proving, again and again, that the team can hold it together.

## How jealousy is processed inside

- **Recognise the trigger.** Notice the early signals — a tightening in the stomach, a hollow ache in the chest, a heat rising.
- **Pause and breathe.** [[emotional-self-regulation|Emotional self-regulation]] means not dumping the emotional wobble on someone else as though it is their job to fix it. Pause. Breathe through the heart centre. Ask: *What is this really about?*
- **Distinguish the feelings.** Jealousy (fear of losing) from envy (wanting what someone else has) from old shame (the echo of a past wound). Each has a different message and a different solution.
- **Sit with discomfort.** A certain amount of discomfort is the price of growth. The complex network does not offer safety by avoiding difficult feelings. It offers safety by proving the team can survive them.
- **Separate past from present.** The ex who used you is not your current partner. The wound of being left is not evidence of being abandoned now. Disentangling these is the heart of [[shadow-work|inner shadow work]].

## Key terms

- **Team process** — the shared, collaborative approach to handling jealousy. Never one's problem to solve alone; a responsibility of everyone involved.
- **Compersion** — the ability to feel authentic joy in a partner's joy with someone else. Not the absence of jealousy; an active, cultivated skill that can coexist with fear. See [[non-attachment-and-compersion]].
- **Fear alarm** — jealousy understood as a signal rather than a truth. It tells you where to look, not what is actually happening.
- **Shadow-work** — the inner work of meeting old wounds and unconscious patterns with conscious curiosity, rather than projecting them onto present relationships. See [[shadow-work]].
- **Heart-centred breathing** — the foundational practice for emotional self-regulation: breathing through the heart centre to return to a centred state before engaging with others. See [[heart-centred-communication]].
