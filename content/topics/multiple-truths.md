# Multiple Truths

## Definition

Multiple truths is the capacity to hold several, seemingly opposing viewpoints as simultaneously valid. Binary perception—good or bad, right or wrong—feeds the inner protector's craving for certainty and builds the fortress: straight walls keeping ambiguity out. Living from the heart hands you a kaleidoscope. Every shift in perspective reveals a new, equally valid pattern of truth. What seems right from one angle appears limited from another. This is not a loss of truth but an expansion into a richer reality in which multiple truths coexist.

## The kaleidoscope of truth

The heart's kaleidoscope unfolds a world where a supposed villain is a wounded soul and a mistake signals an unmet need. Every story can be seen from many different angles. This is not relativism. It is a recognition that actions rarely have a single cause. Behind harm sits a wound, a disconnection, or a lack of emotional, mental or spiritual resources. The heart sees the whole being, the whole story and the whole system around it. Finding stability here means standing not in rigid answers but in compassionate presence.

## How it shows up

- Learning: traditional schooling often operates as a system of right answers, sorted by grades and ranked against peers, where mistakes are marked in red and curiosity outside the curriculum is discouraged. A learning garden nurtures the whole human being, prioritising curiosity over correctness, recognising that seemingly incorrect answers reveal a child's unique learning process. Mistakes become fertiliser for authentic growth.
- Justice: the ego-based legal system asks, "What punishment do they deserve?"—a retributive question focused on blame. The heart asks, "What does this situation need to heal?" A restorative approach focuses on repairing harm, hearing those who were harmed, enabling those who caused harm to understand the impact of their actions, and reintegrating everyone through empathy, accountability and healing.
